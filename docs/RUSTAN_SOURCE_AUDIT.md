# Rustan source-page audit

Review date: 25 October 2026.

## Source and scope

Rustan, R. Agne (1992), *Burden, spacing and borehole diameter at rock blasting*,
International Journal of Surface Mining, Reclamation and Environment 6(3),
141–149. DOI: [10.1080/09208119208944329](https://doi.org/10.1080/09208119208944329).

Printed pages 146 and 147 were visually inspected in images supplied by the
project author from the institutional reprint. They are marked E6 and E7 in
Paper E of Rustan's 1995 doctoral thesis, *Controlled fragmentation and contours
in rock blasting: theoretical and technical approaches*.
[Institutional thesis record](https://ltu.diva-portal.org/smash/record.jsf?pid=diva2:991783).

This review establishes what those two pages print. It does not claim visual
inspection of every page, an author-issued correction, or new field validation.

## Verified readings

| Location | Reading |
|---|---|
| Printed p. 146/E6, Section 4.2, Eq. 18 | `B_p1 = sqrt(B_p * S_p)`. `B_p1` is practical burden for `S/B = 1`; the three lengths are expressed in metres. |
| Printed p. 147/E7, Section 4.3 heading | Open-pit statistical relationship for diameters 89–381 mm. |
| Printed p. 147/E7, Eq. 22 | `B_p1 = 18.1 * d^0.689`. The line below the equation repeats the 89–381 mm interval. |
| Printed p. 147/E7, Figure 5 | The surface-mine label prints `18.8 * d^0.689`, conflicting with Eq. 22's coefficient 18.1. |
| Printed p. 147/E7, Eq. 22 annotations | The labels describe +52% and -37% expected extrema; this page does not assign them a confidence level. |

The normalization equation preserves the product of practical burden and spacing.
Algebraically, if `r = S_p / B_p > 0`, then `B_p = B_p1 / sqrt(r)` and
`S_p = B_p1 * sqrt(r)`. These are consequences of Eq. 18 and the definition of
`r`, not additional calibrated design equations implemented by this change.

## Reconstruction decisions

The implementation follows the explicit numbered Eq. 22 and retains **18.1**.
The conflicting **18.8** figure label is recorded as an unresolved internal
source discrepancy. This review does not establish which value the author
intended for the figure, nor assume a published erratum exists.

`rustan_burden` continues to convert its millimetre argument to metres before
evaluating the existing expression. Its equation, returned numerical values,
and reconstructed 251 mm benchmark remain unchanged. The returned quantity is
the normalized practical burden `B_p1`, not a spacing-independent prescription
for arbitrary blast geometry.

`rustan_applicability` is corrected from the thesis-derived 89–311 mm screen to
the **89–381 mm interval printed in Section 4.3 and below Eq. 22**. Both endpoints
are included. Being inside this diameter interval does not establish all other
source conditions or predictive accuracy at a particular mine.

## Compatibility and research outputs

This is an explicitly documented applicability-screen correction. Calls between
311 and 381 mm change from an extrapolation label to an inside-range label.
The human-readable inside-range and upper-extrapolation messages also change
their reported upper bound. Public function names, signatures, physical input
units, and DataFrame schemas are retained. This behavioral change must be
included in the next release notes; it is not hidden by recapturing the API baseline.

The historical all-model diameter comparison retains its existing grid from
181 through 311 mm and its existing custom-input condition `180 < d_mm <= 311`.
The 311 mm study limit remains separate from Rustan's published 381 mm limit.
Its docstring and runtime warning explain that distinction.

No existing CSV, JSON, notebook, figure, reference input, or numerical artifact
is regenerated by this correction. In particular, the ten checksum-verified
legacy CSVs remain historical records, including their earlier applicability
wording. The released v1.0.0 tag and compatibility baseline are preserved.

The seven-model ensemble remains a comparison of reconstructed legacy equation
outputs. Rustan's normalized practical quantity must be stated when interpreting
the comparison. The source definitions and charging/geometry assumptions for all
seven relationships have not yet been fully harmonized. The calculated spread
therefore does not establish agreement or disagreement between seven independently
validated estimates of an identical operational design target.

## Verification

Six source-based boundary cases cover the lower limit, the former upper limit,
the newly included interval, and the published upper limit. A seventh regression
check preserves the historical grid, its custom-input limit, and its research
decision gate. Existing benchmark and artifact-consistency tests remain the
checks on unchanged numerical outputs. The full real repository suite must pass
before committing the correction.

The preparation helper changes only the reviewed source/documentation sections
and the new tests. It checks that research data, notebooks, version metadata,
and the compatibility baseline retain their existing bytes.
